## Legitimate remaining scoring investigations

### Winnipeg's competitiveness boundary and history allocation

The sampled 11 to 88 change is real, but the available denser trajectory breaks it into smaller recorded steps; the largest adjacent retained change is 41 points. At two goals down with 1:57 left, base 15, live bonus zero and a negative trajectory adjustment produced 11. At one goal down with 0:59, base 53, live bonus 35, a pulled goalie and trailing pressure produced a competitive target near 87 plus carried context. Several inputs changed together. Neither the single goal nor an arbitrary discontinuity rule explains all 77 sampled points.

At 4-4/0:31 the 87 snapshot had `ledger-behind-scoreboard` partial evidence and no recovered-deficit credit. A later retained bucket at the same score and clock rated 92 with a reconciled ledger and four-goal recovery recognized. The bucket timestamps are 30 seconds apart; that is not an exact processing-latency measurement.

The shared history allowance remains a useful calibration hypothesis. At that complete tied state, raw late-tying credit of nine and erased-deficit credit of ten shared about 6.315 points of remaining headroom. Ordered allocation retained the tying credit first and zero additional erased-deficit credit. The model recognized the recovery, but whether this allowance sufficiently distinguishes extraordinary from ordinary recovery warrants a bounded same-model replay.

A useful experiment would independently vary the viability boundary and history allocation across two-goal/one-goal/tied states, 120/60/30-second clocks, verified extra-attacker presence and ordinary versus four-goal recoveries. Controls must include settled blowouts, ordinary ties, overtime and shootouts. No replacement score or production adjustment is established by this investigation.

### Input recovery and viewing usefulness

Dallas shows that missing summary detail and three-minute sampling can obscure an earlier high rating. Its retained 82 to 88 change at the same 1:02 clock coincides with availability of complete score-progression evidence, goalie pull and trailing-chance context. This is evidence for investigating input completeness and observation cadence, not permission to increase provider calls or change notifications.

The two recovered active-game stale-source findings remain valid data-quality concerns. The audit also retains genuine deterministic protection for invalid ratings/scores, corrupt stored state, contradictory model attribution and invalid lifecycle/history. Invalid or stale observations remain in the raw evidence but cannot establish a calibration peak or first valid threshold crossing.

## Implemented capabilities

The audit now separates pregame, live, completed and decided-sudden-death ratings; reports live peaks independently; and retains decisive moments and quantitative comeback evidence. Resolved ratings cannot retroactively establish live underrating.

Peer matching requires sport, exact model, period, actual clock and margin, rejects known context mismatches, requires at least two available context dimensions, and needs five independent games before using a median. It reports match coverage and missing dimensions rather than manufacturing confidence from a broad storage bucket.

Event response supports signed changes, unchanged ratings, context-dependent direction, before/after references, simultaneous-event confounding through the first changed rating, and source limitations. Later cached provider summaries are analyzed as a separate retrospective source, with original occurrence, acquisition and review times. They never backfill historical observations or establish historical input availability. Invalid first post-event states cannot hide a later valid same-model bracket; snapshot buckets already reflecting a goal cannot become its pre-event baseline. Explicit shootout semantics preserve multiple overtime periods. Timestamped provider events are kept separately from state-change intervals. Missing timestamps stay missing; exact provider/publication/polling/scorer latencies remain unavailable.

Threshold analysis covers inclusive 70/80/85/90/95 crossings, game-clock runway, observed episodes, sampled time, continuity uncertainty, decided-state exclusion and separate audit-only versus denser retained crossings. The legacy strictly-greater-than-85 duration remains labeled separately. Invalid observations and intervening model versions censor continuity rather than implying a measured exit or uninterrupted eligibility.

Volatility retains major-change component differences and applicable telemetry, while distinguishing corrections and invalid-source changes from calibration evidence. Repeated unchanged comeback observations are summarized with counts, first/last references and rating range; every underlying rating remains in the trajectory.

Calibration is sport-specific and model-specific, with Grand Slam tennis eligibility and hidden-Soccer prioritization handled explicitly. Cross-version overview distributions are withheld. Empty sports, short samples and the absence of an objective excitement benchmark prevent a one-night calibration verdict.

Recommendations include the requested game/time/rating, verified situation, reason for review, components, mechanism, support, peers, gaps, confidence, user impact, proposed investigation, expected effects on other scenarios and replay requirement. Classifications distinguish confirmed defects, strong concerns, hypotheses, evidence limitations, expected behavior and insufficient evidence. There are no automatically implemented scoring changes or invented target scores.

The protected dashboard and two-entry nightly ZIP expose these capabilities while preserving authentication, historical finding/recovery records, slate-date grouping and download filenames. Schema version 3 describes the retained-data projection and bounds. Source references identify exact audit or scoring-snapshot rows.

## Remaining evidence limitations

- Actual provider publication time, canonical commit time, arrival history and notification delivery logs are not established. `feed.updatedAt` is refresh start; live snapshot times are floored refresh-start buckets.
- A later cached summary may supply event identity and occurrence time without proving when the live scorer received that event, or that the earlier cached revision was identical.
- Historical score-calculation ledgers are not universally retained. Components and factors do not always form a complete additive reconstruction through floors, caps, ordering and rounding.
- Some possession, manpower, timeout, market, pressure and tennis-point fields remain unavailable. Producer-complete score progression is not complete audit context.
- One retained night cannot establish historical rarity, systematic cross-sport miscalibration, an objectively correct target score, or whether 85 is the best future posting/alert threshold.
- Observation retention and explicit read limits can prevent complete reconstruction. Missing intervals and truncation are disclosed; neither is filled with fabricated data.

The upgraded audit can support specific, evidence-linked investigations and bounded replay experiments. It does not yet justify autonomous scoring corrections. Stronger claims require multi-night same-model cohorts, reliable event/input provenance, and replay of complete component interactions.
