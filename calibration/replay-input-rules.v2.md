# Offline replay input rules v2

This repair changes offline replay and bounded review generation only. `scripts/shadow-candidate-report.mjs` exports corrected checkpoints by default. Explicit historical reproduction imports `scripts/replay-inputs.historical.mjs`, which preserves the prior faulty reconstruction. The old generators, frozen checkpoint hash assertions, reports, and staging manifest continue to describe v1. New evaluation uses `scripts/replay-input-comparison.mjs`; it does not run either calibration grid.

## Ordering evidence

The retained source is ESPN's public site API. Basketball and football clocks count down; NHL clocks count up. MLB uses inning/half and source pitch order; at-bat sequence numbers restart. Primary football records come from drives, with summary/scoring records added by identity. Conflicting duplicate state is quarantined. Missing stable identity or unpositioned events are excluded explicitly.

Agreement between sport clock and retained source order establishes order even if sequence numbers or wall timestamps disagree. Period precedes normalized time, so an end-of-period record precedes the next period even when their elapsed positions coincide. Equal-clock records within a period retain meaningful source order; sequence numbers do not break ties. A source/clock inversion is reordered only with corroborating wall time, or (only for NFL/NHL, whose retained sequences support game-wide order) clock/sequence agreement when wall evidence is absent. Opposing wall evidence does not count as absent. Basketball and CFB sequence metadata cannot independently resolve a source/clock conflict. If these rules cannot resolve a conflict, both conflicting records are quarantined. The scope artifact records the pair and evidence; no event-ID exceptions exist.

This is a conservative partial reconstruction, not a claim that any one time field is always right. In particular, a football clock increase may be a real clock adjustment, stale metadata, or a provider error. Without enough evidence, this implementation excludes the conflicting records instead of inventing an adjustment.

## Scores and causal membership

Scores are copied literally from admitted records; missing scores remain missing until supported. A rollback is accepted literally only with an explicit score-correction flag/type. An unexplained rollback quarantines its interval. A later complete scoring snapshot consistent with the last supported snapshot may restart evaluation, but cannot retroactively explain the excluded interval. Every later checkpoint records the omitted earlier IDs and incomplete-history limitation. No record's score is replaced with a cumulative maximum, and score comparisons never determine event order.

Accepted plays, scoring plays, rolling scores, WP and partial drives are rebuilt from the same accepted prefix. Archived drive outcome/end/totals metadata is stripped. Completed prior drive results (including punts) are reconstructed from accepted terminal event types only when the whole drive is accepted. Incomplete groups remain in `drives.incomplete`; they never become zero-punt observations in the scorer's completed-drive denominator. Current-drive results stay absent. No later scoring-event flag is attached to a checkpoint. A play-linked WP observation requires its play in the accepted prefix; an unknown/excluded play ID never falls back to clock matching. Its own time and period, if supplied, must also precede or equal the checkpoint. A timestamp later than the checkpoint is excluded. Clock-only WP waits until strictly past that clock, because its place within equal-clock events is unknown. This rule does not depend on how many later events are appended to that group.

Appending unrelated later events cannot reorder an established supported prefix. Contradictory later evidence is a different case: it may expose ambiguity in retrospective records and cause quarantine. Tests cover unrelated append stability, future-event/WP exclusion, same-clock order, sport clock conventions, and partial drives whose retained source ordering required repair.

## Football situation validation

`validateFootballSituation` is the single offline validation boundary. A scrimmage endpoint needs a down from 1 through 4, a supplied nonnegative distance, a supplied distance to end zone greater than zero and at most 100, and a participating team. Possession additionally needs explicit after-play possession or matching start/end teams on an identifiable continuing scrimmage event (rush, reception, incompletion, sack, penalty, or own-fumble recovery). Endpoint team alone is insufficient. Missing numeric values are never coerced to zero. Red-zone evidence is derived only after scrimmage validation.

Scoring, completed try, kickoff/punt/turnover and period-end records are separate states with no active scrimmage projection. An explicit after-record pending two-point flag with a supported team preserves a try state independently of down validity. Touchdown prose mentioning a PAT does not establish a pending try.

A timeout can retain a supported continuing scrimmage situation if its supplied endpoint fields agree, no excluded intervening record breaks continuity, and the drive identity has not changed. Missing timeout endpoint fields may retain established values; contradictory fields may not. A timeout after a touchdown, turnover, kickoff, period boundary or unresolved transition does not inherit the old possession. No later kickoff or description is used to settle the timeout. Missing possession leaves score, clock and supported event history available to the frozen scorer.

## Interpretation and production boundary

The 107 retained captures are retrospective. They cannot establish original live availability, publication latency, a provider revision log, or that scoring descriptions had their captured wording at the event time. Corrections here establish support within the archive only. Review cards with unresolved current situations or earlier excluded history stay explicitly excluded from confident preference judgments even when score/clock diagnostics remain evaluable.

Production adapters, public scoring APIs, default trajectory-off behavior and completed scoring are unchanged. The shared football scorer still trusts malformed possession/field-position inputs from other callers; this replay-only fix does not harden those callers. A separate proposal is a shared validated-current-situation contract guarding active drive/floor logic in shared football/NFL/CFB scoring, while preserving valid timeout continuity and explicit tries. That behavior change is not enabled here.

Frozen policies remain Phase 2 modifier 65/35 with ceiling 34, Generic Leverage 8 / 0.55 / 0.40, and the existing Phase 3/3.1 definitions. The bounded comparison evaluates the same corrected input under the five requested policies, separates matched input effects from policy differences, and reports added/removed/excluded identities without preserving the old checkpoint count by force.

The scope artifact distinguishes record-level findings from checkpoint-prefix changes. `unaffectedPlayIds` means no direct repair or exclusion was applied to that record; its checkpoint may still change because earlier evidence changed. Ordering evidence and unresolved findings can overlap on one record: an ordering repair does not resolve a separate score conflict, and exclusion takes precedence. Football projection repair likewise does not establish the actual unknown possession. The `changes`, `exclusions`, and situation reason fields preserve these distinctions rather than counting every missing/invalid down as one defect.
