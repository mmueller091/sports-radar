About SlateMeter

Think about a Saturday afternoon during college football season. There are 15 games on, you don't have a rooting interest, and you just want to find a good game to watch. Which one do you pick?

The biggest matchup isn't necessarily the most entertaining, and a close score doesn't always mean a good game. That's really where the idea for SlateMeter came from.

How it works

SlateMeter rates sporting events on a scale of 0 to 100 based on how exciting they're likely to be, or how exciting they've become.

Before a game starts, it looks at things like team quality, records, rivalries, and what's at stake. Once the game begins, those ratings continuously update based on what's actually happening.

A big comeback, a late scoring opportunity, overtime, or a particularly competitive matchup can all make a game more interesting. And because every sport is different, SlateMeter uses different scoring models for football, basketball, hockey, and baseball.

The goal isn't to predict who's going to win. It's to help answer a much simpler question: What's worth watching right now?

Who's behind it?

I'm Matt. I'm a lifelong sports fan, and I work with data and analytics for a living. SlateMeter is a side project that combines those two interests.

There's no development team or big company behind it. I build and maintain it myself in my spare time.

I don't think there's ever going to be a perfect formula for excitement. Sports are too unpredictable, and people have different opinions about what makes a game great.

But I do think it's possible to build something that gets pretty good at identifying games worth watching.

Some ratings will be wrong. Some games will be more exciting than the numbers suggest, and others won't live up to their expectations. I'm constantly looking at what the models get right, what they miss, and how they can improve.

If SlateMeter gets you to turn on a game between two teams you normally wouldn't pay attention to, and it turns out to be one of the best games of the day, that's exactly what I built it for.

Install SlateMeter

Add SlateMeter to your Home Screen for its own icon and app window. On iPhone, installation is required for notifications. Once enabled, background pushes can arrive while the app is closed.

iPhone · Safari
  1. Open SlateMeter in Safari.
  2. Tap Share — the square with an upward arrow .
  3. Select Add to Home Screen.
  4. Enable Open as Web App.
  5. Tap Add.
  6. Open SlateMeter using its new Home Screen icon.
  7. Open Settings > Notifications in SlateMeter.
  8. Enable notifications and grant permission.
  9. Select preferred sports and teams.
Android · Chrome ⋮
  1. Open SlateMeter in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three-dot menu ⋮.
  3. Select Install app, Install and create shortcut, or Add to Home screen, depending on Chrome.
  4. Confirm installation.
  5. Open SlateMeter from its icon.
  6. Open Settings > Notifications in SlateMeter.
  7. Enable notifications and choose sports and teams.

UNDER THE HOOD

How the rating works.

Live scores and game details come from public ESPN feeds and refresh each minute. NBA and NHL schedules include yesterday, today, and the following seven days. If the initial feed cannot be reached, retry from the status line. Later refresh failures keep the last loaded scores and mark them stale.

Before playMatchup stakes, team quality, rankings, and market expectations. NFL division positioning uses actual standings; recent form, scoring potential, and verified stories add bounded context.
During playCurrent leverage leads the rating: margin, time remaining, possession or baserunners, and verified recent momentum. Pregame reputation fades after play begins.
After playThe whole game matters: final margin, sustained competitiveness, comebacks, lead changes, overtime, and the deciding sequence when available.
Betting linesShown when ESPN lists them. MLB and NHL may offer a moneyline rather than a run/puck spread. Post-start pregame lines are not labeled as verified closes unless the feed says so.
Ranking modelMomentum signals are capped so repeated descriptions of the same run do not dominate the rating. The 1–99 score is an estimate, not a league statistic.
SCORE GUIDE

What the ratings mean.

The number answers a different question before, during, and after a game: how promising it looks, how urgently it is worth watching now, or how exciting the full game was.

95–99Exceptional Historic drama, a remarkable finish, or something rare unfolding.
85–94Tune in now A high-leverage game with a strong reason to watch immediately.
70–84Strong watch A compelling matchup, live situation, or finished game.
50–69Worth checking Competitive or notable, but not yet urgent.
1–49Background Limited stakes, an ordinary situation, or a game moving toward a clear result.

A quiet slate may have no games above 85. Scores are not forced into every range.
